# Roast Whiff: one note at a time

## Creative direction

Lead with one sticky note. The joke is the content; the exact-note link is the invitation to explore. Keep the roast landing page minimal: note, one Share action, more roasts, and a small link to Whiff. Explain the product on the main site, not inside the roast campaign.

Editorial jokes have no invented customer identity or testimonial claim. Community notes retain the names their authors submitted and the existing approval process. Internally, preserve the distinction so authored jokes are never counted as user reviews. Avoid external posts that falsely claim to be independent customers.

## One way to pass a note

Use **Share** on any note. Where supported, it opens the device’s share menu
with the quote and exact-note URL. Otherwise it copies the roast and link,
ready to paste into a post or group chat. If copying is blocked, selectable
text appears for manual copying. Cancelling a share does nothing.

Each quote on the wall also opens its individual note. Social platforms that
support link previews can display that note’s generated sticky-note image.
Share one thought, not a screenshot of the whole page: recipients should
recognize the same note when they open its link.

## Short caption drafts

> Making friends apparently comes with a syllabus. [note link]

> A twelve-week commitment? My houseplants would like a word. [note link]

> This one belongs on the wall. [note link]

Use these from the Whiff or founder account. Let the joke create curiosity without an invented claim about what customers have said. Respect the rules of each community and avoid repetitive promotional posts.

## What to measure

Compare visits to individual notes, clicks onward to Whiff, and completed invite requests. A share button records intent, not proof that someone published a post. Use placement-specific UTM parameters in externally distributed links if desired, without personal information. Verify attribution across the journey before claiming which roast produced signups.

## Launch checks

The existing production community API returned HTTP 503 during the previous review. Editorial notes can remain readable without that service, but real submissions and moderation still need functioning production storage. Test the real published URLs, native sharing, clipboard fallback, social previews and a controlled moderation cycle after deployment. Nothing in this local work publishes posts or deploys the site.
